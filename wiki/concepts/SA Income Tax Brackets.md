South Africa's personal income tax uses 7 progressive marginal brackets set annually by SARS (usually in the February Budget Speech), applied to taxable income for the tax year running 1 March–28/29 February.

## Core Ideas

**2025/26 tax year brackets** (1 March 2025 – 28 February 2026) — SARS did **not** adjust brackets for inflation this year, so 2025/26 and the prior 2024/25 year use identical thresholds and rates:

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| R1 – R237,100 | 18% |
| R237,101 – R370,500 | 26% |
| R370,501 – R512,800 | 31% |
| R512,801 – R673,000 | 36% |
| R673,001 – R857,900 | 39% |
| R857,901 – R1,817,000 | **41%** |
| Above R1,817,000 | 45% |

**Tax thresholds** (no tax payable below): R95,750 (under 65), R148,217 (65–74), R165,689 (75+).

**Rebates**: Primary R17,235 · Secondary (65+) R9,444 · Tertiary (75+) R3,145.

**Key mechanic**: brackets are marginal, not flat — only the portion of income within a band is taxed at that band's rate. A single additional payment (e.g. a bonus) is taxed at the earner's *top* marginal rate for that year, since it lands on top of already-earned income.

**Bracket creep**: because brackets aren't guaranteed to move with inflation every year (as in 2025/26), a person's real tax burden can rise even with no rate change, if their income grows but their bracket doesn't move.
