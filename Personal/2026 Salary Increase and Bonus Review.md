# 2026 Salary Increase and Bonus Review

Source data: IRP5 tax certificates 2023–2026 (Income code 3601 = base salary, Annual Payment code 3605 = bonus). Employer: Powerfleet.

## Table

| Year | % SA CPI | % Salary Increase | Real Gain | % Bonus of Base | % Bonus Δ vs LY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022/23 | 7.04% | — | — | 8.91% | — |
| 2023/24 | 6.08% | 7.70% | +1.62 pp | 9.37% | +13.33% |
| 2024/25 | 4.36% | 5.80% | +1.44 pp | 8.80% | -0.74% |
| 2025/26 | 3.22% | 5.02% | +1.80 pp | 8.74% | +4.38% |
| **2026/27 (proposed)** | ~3.2%* | 4.00% | **+0.80 pp** | **1.81%** | **-78.43%** |

\* 2026 full calendar-year CPI not yet published; using 2025's 3.22% as the closest available reference. Tax years run Mar–Feb, mapped to the calendar year they mostly overlap.

## Takeaways

- Salary increases have beaten CPI every year, but the proposed 2026/27 real gain (+0.80 pp) is less than half of last year's (+1.80 pp) — a real, not just perceived, step down in how much the raise outpaces inflation.
- Bonus has held remarkably steady at 8.7–9.4% of base salary for four straight years. The proposed R19,000 bonus is only 1.81% of base — a ~78% cut in absolute terms and a completely different order of magnitude from the established pattern, not just a bad year.

## Note on tax treatment

All figures above are **gross (pre-tax)**, taken directly from IRP5 codes 3601 (Income) and 3605 (Annual Payment) — the certificate's PAYE line (4102) is a single combined tax figure covering salary + bonus + fringe benefits, so tax can't be isolated per income type from the certificate alone. This doesn't distort the comparison: gross taxable income (code 3699) has been above R1M every year in this dataset, so the same top marginal bracket (39–41%, per SARS 2025/26 tables — 41% band is R857,901–R1,817,000, not the 45% band which starts at R1,817,001) has applied throughout. A constant marginal rate across years means the gross % changes shown (bonus as % of base, bonus Δ YoY) hold equally in net terms — e.g. the proposed R19,000 bonus is ~R11,210 after tax at 41%, but the ~78% cut vs. last year is the same whether measured gross or net.
